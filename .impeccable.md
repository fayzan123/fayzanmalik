## Design Context

### Users
Primarily technical recruiters, hiring managers, and potential collaborators — people who are evaluating Fayzan as a builder. They arrive with a specific job: assess whether this person is worth reaching out to. They're often scanning quickly; the site needs to establish credibility in the first 3 seconds, then reward deeper exploration. Secondary audience: peers in the developer/AI community who may discover the site via GitHub or social.

### Brand Personality
**Precise. Shipped. Technical.**

Voice is a mix of casual confidence and professional credibility — the kind of developer who writes clean commit messages but also types `sudo hire fayzan` as an easter egg. Copy in section headers and labels is clean and direct. Bio and descriptive text can have a human voice. Never corporate, never try-hard.

Emotional goal: visitors should leave thinking "this person builds real things, ships them, and knows what they're doing" — while also wanting to reach out.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Theme:** Dark only (initially). Near-black background (`#0a0a0a`), not pure black. The darkness is a canvas, not a void.
- **Accent:** Terminal green (`#00ff88`) — used sparingly and with restraint. It signals "this person lives in a terminal" without screaming it.
- **Typography:** Inter for all prose (headings, body, CTAs). JetBrains Mono for anything technical — tags, dates, labels, section prefixes, the navbar logo. The font contrast between Inter and JetBrains Mono is a deliberate design choice, not an accident.
- **References:** itaisigler.com — minimal, confident, text-forward. The site respects the visitor's attention.
- **Anti-references:** Generic AI-generated portfolios. Symptoms: gradient-heavy hero sections, neon glow on everything, excessive card shadows, overcrowded skill badges, "I'm passionate about..." copy, animations that serve no purpose, hover effects on elements that don't need them.
- **Motion:** Purposeful only. Scroll reveals should feel like content loading in, not a magic show. Hover states confirm interactivity, not perform it. The CTA pulse draws the eye once; it doesn't distract.

### Design Principles

1. **The CLI earns the right to be there.** The terminal in the hero is not a gimmick — it's the product. Every design decision around it should make it feel at home, not showcased.

2. **Restraint is the aesthetic.** Less shadow. Less color. Less animation. If removing an element makes the design stronger, remove it. Whitespace (darkspace) is structure, not emptiness.

3. **Technical detail as texture.** JetBrains Mono for dates, tags, and labels. The `data-animate` stagger. The accent ring on the headshot. These micro-details signal craft without announcing it.

4. **Credibility before personality.** The first pass of any section should read as competent and clear. Personality (the casual bio, the easter egg, the terminal) layers on top — it never substitutes for substance.

5. **Every interaction confirms, never surprises.** Hover states, scroll animations, and transitions should feel like natural responses to user intent. Nothing should animate unexpectedly or demand attention.
